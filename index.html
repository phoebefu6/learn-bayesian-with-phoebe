Learn Bayesian with Phoebe

Halloway Bakeries' smallest outlet redeemed one voucher in eighteen. Its true redemption rate is 0.192, the highest in the chain. Head office's sensible prior, worth a hundred pseudo-observations, pulled the estimate to 0.110 with an interval that did not contain the truth; a prior learned from the other eleven outlets got it to 0.124 and saved the site from closure. Everything that follows comes from the gap between those numbers: what a prior is worth and what it costs, why small data give you a distribution rather than a point, how eleven outlets can lend the twelfth their evidence, and when a probability becomes a decision. Six leader sessions with no code, ten practitioner sessions on PyMC 6 as it is in 2026, and three benches on a world whose truth is written down so every method can be scored.

Leader track 🎲 · six sessions, no code

For whoever signs off a number from small data. What a prior costs, why eighteen observations give you a distribution and not a point, how to borrow strength across units, how to read a posterior in a meeting, the argument about priors the field has not settled, and what to require in writing.

🎲 Leader session 1 · start here

What a prior costs

A prior is a number of pseudo-observations. At Northgate the sensible one was confidently wrong; at Westfield a wrong one still moved the answer by 0.026 at n = 900.

▶ Start here 🔍 Leader session 2

Small data, honest uncertainty

Eighteen observations cannot give you a point. What they can give you is a distribution, and the sentence that goes on the slide.

 🏪 Leader session 3

Borrowing strength

Twelve small outlets know more together than any one alone. The pooling ladder, 0.054 to 0.031 to 0.025, and the six outlets it made worse.

Reading a posterior

P(B beats A), expected loss, and why 0.915 was not enough to stop. Credible against confidence, in one sentence.

The prior argument

Subjective, reference, weakly informative. The field has not settled it, and this session does not pretend to.

Commissioning a Bayesian answer

Practitioner track 🧪 · ten sessions, hands on

For the people who build the models. Conjugate updates by hand, the posterior as a distribution, MCMC and its diagnostics, PyMC 6 with nutpie and DataTree, hierarchical models, Bayesian A/B by expected loss, predictive checks, the executive sentence, and a capstone bench scored against the truth.

 🔗 Practitioner session 4

MCMC and its diagnostics

Hierarchical models

Bayesian A/B decisions

P(B beats A), expected loss, ROPE. The rule stops at n = 100 and is wrong 2 times in 200, counted.

Checking the model

Prior and posterior predictive checks. What a check can catch, what it cannot, and where LOO fits.

Saying it to executives
